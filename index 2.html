Should I list or take a cash offer? A DFW agent's honest breakdown

By Cristian Velez · Licensed Realtor, Corey Simpson & Associates · Principal, Elite Offer Group

If you're a homeowner in the Dallas-Fort Worth area thinking about selling, you've probably been approached by a cash buyer or seen the ads. "We buy houses fast." "No repairs, no showings, close in two weeks." At the same time, you've probably heard that listing on the MLS will get you top dollar.

So which one is right? The honest answer: it depends on your situation. And most people giving you that advice are only selling you one of the two options. I do both, which means I can give you a straight comparison.

What a cash offer actually means

A cash offer from an investor or acquisition company is designed for speed and certainty. You skip the showings, the repairs, the negotiations with retail buyers, and the 30-to-45-day escrow process. You get a set number, and you close, often in two to three weeks.

The tradeoff is price. Cash buyers are pricing in their risk, their rehab costs, and their profit margin. In most DFW submarkets right now, a cash offer will come in 10 to 25 percent below what a well-priced listing could fetch. That gap is the cost of convenience and certainty.

Cash offers make sense when: you need to move fast, the property needs significant work, you're dealing with a difficult personal situation (divorce, foreclosure, inherited property), or the carrying costs of holding the property outweigh the potential upside of listing.

What listing on the MLS actually means

A traditional listing, done right, gets your property in front of the widest possible buyer pool, including financed buyers who can often pay more than investors because they're not factoring in a rehab margin or a flip profit.

In strong Collin County submarkets like Plano, McKinney, and Frisco, a well-priced, well-presented listing can still move in days and attract multiple offers. The process takes longer, typically 45 to 60 days from list to close, and requires some preparation: repairs, staging, photography, showings.

Listing makes sense when: the property is in decent condition, you have time to run the process properly, and maximizing net proceeds is the primary goal.

The option most sellers don't know they have

Because I operate on both sides, as a licensed agent and as a cash buyer through Elite Offer Group, I can give sellers something most people in this market can't: a genuine side-by-side comparison with no agenda.

In some cases, the right answer is a hybrid: get a cash offer number to anchor your floor, then list and see what the market brings. If the MLS produces strong offers, you take the best one. If it doesn't, you have a cash offer ready to go. You're never stuck.

The bottom line

There's no universally right answer. What matters is understanding your actual options, the real numbers behind each one, and what your specific situation requires. If you're in the DFW area and thinking about selling, in any condition or on any timeline, I'm happy to walk through both paths and give you an honest take on which one makes more sense.

No pressure, no pitch. Just a straight conversation about your situation.
